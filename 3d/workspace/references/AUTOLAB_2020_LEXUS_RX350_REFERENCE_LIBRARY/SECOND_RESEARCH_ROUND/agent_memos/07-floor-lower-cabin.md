# 2020 Lexus RX 350 AWD (five-seat RX) — floor and lower-cabin reference memo

## Scope and identification

This memo targets the conventional five-seat fourth-generation RX, specifically the 2020 Lexus RX 350 AWD rather than the longer RX 350L. The 2020 U.S. brochure lists the RX 350 AWD as a 3.5-liter V6 / 8-speed automatic AWD variant and describes the standard RX seating as power-adjustable front seats plus a 40/20/40-split folding, reclining second row [1]. The brochure also distinguishes RX F SPORT equipment from the ordinary RX 350, including aluminum pedals and door-sill scuff plates as F SPORT additions [1]. Therefore, F SPORT pedal/sill appearance must not be generalized to every RX 350 AWD.

Evidence classes used here are: **A** = manufacturer or OEM catalog/technical source; **B** = repair/dismantling record or owner repair discussion; **C** = dealer/commercial parts listing; **D** = secondary or weakly inspectable reference. Public availability is not treated as commercial reuse permission.

## Findings by requested area

### Footwell, carpet, and mats

The strongest exact-fit evidence is the authorized Lexus dealer parts page for “Carpet Floor Mats - Black.” Its product specification identifies four pieces, part number PT208-24151-22, black, with logo, and AWD transmission fitment. It states that the accessory protects the original carpet, uses nibbed backing, and has quarter-turn fasteners on the driver and front-passenger mats. The same page warns that mats are model/year specific, must be secured to avoid interference with pedal operation, and must not be stacked over another liner or mat [2]. This establishes the visible/technical relationship between removable mats, factory carpet, retention points, and the pedal-clearance safety envelope. It does **not** establish the shape or dimensions of the underlying carpet, nor does it show a bare footwell.

The 2020 OEM parts catalog separately lists “Carpet Floor Mats” as a 2020 RX350 interior category and describes a four-piece F SPORT set with nibbed backing and quarter-turn fasteners. The listing is useful as an OEM nomenclature/fitment corroboration, but the catalog page is not a factory service-manual illustration of the installed carpet [3].

The 2020 brochure confirms NuLuxe/leather interior offerings and power-adjustable front seats, but it does not provide a carpet cut pattern or footwell construction drawing [1].

### Center tunnel and lower center-console trim

The 2020 OEM catalog has a dedicated “Center Console” assembly for the 2020 RX350 base 3.5L V6. It divides the assembly into Console Body, Shifter Assembly, Left, and Courtesy Lamp diagrams, and lists console-body variants by interior color [4]. This establishes that the center tunnel is treated as a multi-part console assembly rather than a single undifferentiated piece. It is useful for locating the console body, shifter surround, side pieces, and lighting-related parts, but the extracted page does not expose a complete exploded image or removal sequence.

A fourth-generation RX owner repair thread provides direct dismantling evidence for lower center-console side trim. The author identifies a short driver-side console cover adjacent to a longer panel extending toward the gas pedal. Their removal sequence calls for removing the leather side trims, silver radio surround, media-control unit and its plug, two exposed screws, loosening the cupholder/shift panel, removing the rear vent assembly, then removing the short side cover before the long panel. The author reports that the driver seat had to be removed for sufficient clearance, that the long panel has two screws including one hidden beneath storage, and that the panel pulls toward the operator [5]. This is hands-on repair evidence for access relationships among seat, console side trim, and pedal-side lower trim on the 2016–2022 RX generation. It is not a factory procedure and does not prove that every 2020 trim level uses identical fastener detail.

### Pedals and pedal-side trim

The AWD-specific dealer listing’s warning about securing floor mats to prevent pedal interference is the most direct technical evidence found for the driver footwell/pedal relationship [2]. The 2020 brochure identifies aluminum pedals only with RX F SPORT / F SPORT AWD equipment, not as universal RX 350 AWD equipment [1]. The ordinary RX 350 AWD reference should therefore show conventional pedal treatment unless the target vehicle is specifically F SPORT. No reliable source reviewed here established pedal dimensions, pedal spacing, or a bare-pedal mounting detail; none should be inferred.

### Seat rails and seat-adjacent floor

The 2020 OEM “Driver Seat Components” catalog lists a front-left seat track for sport-seat configuration, left/right track covers for front seats, seat-side finish panels, power-seat switches, and related seat wiring/adjustment components. It also distinguishes “with sport seat” and “without sport seat” configurations and gives multiple market/build/interior variants [6]. This establishes the presence of a front seat-track/rail system, removable track covers, and lower side trim around the seat base. It does not expose rail dimensions, bolt spacing, or a complete underside view.

The repair thread’s statement that the driver seat had to be removed to gain clearance for console-side-panel removal is practical evidence that seat position/clearance is part of the lower-console dismantling path [5]. It is not evidence that the seat rail itself must be removed for carpet removal.

### Sills, rocker, and lower structural trim

The 2020 OEM catalog has a dedicated “Floor” assembly that names the front floor pan, center floor pan, rear sill plate, floor cover, floor-pan reinforcement, and floor side-rail reinforcements. The page distinguishes non-hybrid RX350 structure and notes Canada-built/U.S.-built variants [7]. The parallel “Floor & Rails” category repeats front floor-pan brackets, center/right/left brackets, crossmembers, side reinforcements, and front floor side-rail reinforcements [8]. These entries technically establish the floor’s structural zones and the existence of rear sill plate/floor-cover components, but they are catalog text rather than a visible bare-floor photograph.

The 2020 “Center Pillar & Rocker” catalog lists right/left center-pillar and rocker assemblies, B-pillar reinforcement, inner center-pillar pieces, rocker-panel reinforcement, and inner rocker components [9]. This is strong OEM nomenclature for the sill/rocker structural region. It should not be read as proof that the visible plastic door-sill/scuff trim has the same part grouping; the catalog page is primarily body-structure oriented.

The 2020 brochure states that aluminum pedals and door-sill scuff plates are included with the F SPORT equipment set, while LED-illuminated door sills appear under the Luxury Package [1]. This provides trim-level provenance for visible sill treatments and prevents conflating F SPORT aluminum scuff plates with the standard RX 350 AWD sill finish.

## Coverage and gaps

The source set supports the requested footwell, fixed-carpet relationship, removable mats, center tunnel/console, pedal-clearance constraint, front seat track/rail presence, floor/sill/rocker structure, and lower-console dismantling path. It also distinguishes ordinary RX 350 AWD equipment from F SPORT-specific aluminum pedals/scuff plates and from RX 350L three-row packaging.

Important gaps remain. No reviewed source supplied verified carpet dimensions, floor cross-section thickness, rail bolt spacing, pedal dimensions, sill-clip counts, or a complete 2020 RX 350 AWD bare-cabin teardown. The forum evidence is fourth-generation rather than VIN-specific 2020, and the OEM catalog branches by trim, build market, and hybrid/non-hybrid configuration. Video search results were not used as visual evidence because no inspected video page in this pass provided more than a title/snippet-level basis for the requested components. These gaps should remain explicit in any downstream reference package.
