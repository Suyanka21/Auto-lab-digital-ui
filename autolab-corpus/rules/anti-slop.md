# Anti-Slop Rules

These rules override generic AI design habits.

## Prohibited patterns

Do not use:

- Generic AI landing-page hero sections.
- Centered headline + gradient background + floating 3D blobs.
- Purple-blue gradients.
- Neon glows.
- Glassmorphism.
- Excessive blur or translucent panels.
- Emoji as UI icons.
- Generic “trusted by” logo strips.
- Fake testimonials.
- Fabricated statistics.
- Fake customer reviews.
- Unsupported pricing.
- Generic dashboard cards.
- Equal-width three-column feature grids without a strong editorial reason.
- Decorative animated particles.
- Unnecessary 3D backgrounds.
- Excessive parallax.
- Generic rounded-corner SaaS cards.
- Overly cheerful or corporate stock photography.

## Motion rules

Motion must clarify hierarchy, state, or spatial relationship.

Use:

- 150–300 ms transitions for UI state.
- 400–700 ms transitions for major section entrances.
- Subtle image scale or opacity transitions.
- Clear focus and selected-state feedback.
- Reduced-motion fallbacks.

Avoid:

- Motion for decoration.
- Continuous looping animation.
- Parallax during configuration controls.
- Distracting hover effects.
- Heavy animation libraries.
- Animations that delay access to controls.

## Copy rules

Do not invent:

- Customer testimonials.
- Awards.
- Client counts.
- Years of experience.
- Delivery timelines.
- Prices.
- Availability.
- Guarantees.
- Partnerships.

Use concise, credible automotive-atelier language.

## Genericness audit

Before completion, identify every element that could appear in a generic AI-generated SaaS site and remove, redesign, or replace it.

Ask:

- Would this look at home in a premium automotive atelier?
- Does every visual choice support material, craft, or configurator clarity?
- Is there a reason for this gradient, glow, card, animation, or icon?
- Would a discerning automotive client consider this credible?
