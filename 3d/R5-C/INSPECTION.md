I compared the driver seat with the approved AI primary and the R5-B three-quarter render. The R5-C views use CPU Cycles and the R5-B studio lights. Only the comparison sheet mirrors the presentation to align the visible sides.

## What changed from R5-B

- Both shoulder faces have a local change of curvature where the upper backrest turns into the bolster. Their cut edges stay fixed.
- **Lumbar:** the padded lands between the four draw lines are raised on the `Drawn lumbar channels` key. Setting the key to `0` restores the plain R5-B foundation exactly.
- **Cushion nose:** the centre waterfall has a raised crest above the front bend and a slight relaxation just below it. Outer thigh supports, cut edges and apron line are unchanged.
- Seven leather materials share an editable procedural finish. Perforation is independently enabled on the yoke, lumbar insert and cushion waterfall.
- Both back and both cushion bolsters carry paired tonal stitch rows. A single U-shaped row follows the headrest facing. All five thread objects are removable.

## Remaining visual discrepancies

1. In `sheets/reference.jpg`, the primary's centre inserts stand apart from its plain bolsters even at whole-seat scale. The R5-C perforations soften to a fine texture at that distance. Its cognac also looks lighter and pinker in this lighting. The reference camera, light and physical hide are unknown.
2. In `views/bolster.jpg`, the highlight changes direction at the shoulder. At three-quarter distance that turn looks shallower than in the primary. The preserved headrest also looks squarer.
3. In `views/channels.jpg`, the four recessed lines remain distinct in the padded insert. The inner pair is harder to read in the three-quarter view. The primary has finer seam definition beside the channels and at the curved yoke joint. R5-C has no separate thread runs on either line. The plain control shows the same padding with the perforation switched off.
4. In `views/cushion.jpg`, a highlight rolls down the front of the perforated insert. The highlight still spans most of its width, giving the nose a more uniform curve than the primary.
5. In `views/needlework.jpg`, the seam casts a narrow shadow and the tonal stitches resolve individually. At that magnification, the plain bolster grain has a more regular pebble pattern than the reference suggests.
