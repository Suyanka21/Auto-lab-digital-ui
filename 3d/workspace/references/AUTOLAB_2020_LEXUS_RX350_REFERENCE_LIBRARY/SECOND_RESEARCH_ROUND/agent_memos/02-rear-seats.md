# 2020 Lexus RX 350 AWD (five-seat) — rear-seat reference memo

## Scope and conclusion

This memo covers the conventional-wheelbase 2020 Lexus RX 350 AWD five-seat interior, not the RX 350L. The best-supported configuration is a three-place, 40/20/40-split rear bench with three adjustable rear head restraints, three rear three-point belts, a folding center armrest with cupholders, reclining seatbacks, and a split-fold mechanism that can leave the center section folded independently. The owner’s manual describes the physical release levers and the required stowage of head restraints, the center belt buckle, and belts in side pockets before folding. The official brochure describes the bench as standard and identifies power-folding/reclining as an available feature on the two-row RX 350; the Canadian specification sheet likewise distinguishes standard foldable seats from an available power-folding package. [1] [2] [3]

The cargo-floor relationship is documented as a lift-up carpeted cargo floor/mat over a spare-tire and tool compartment, with the second-row backrests folding from the cargo-side levers. Neither the official sources reviewed gives a claimed flat-floor dimension or a measured seatback-to-floor height. Do not infer a perfectly level load floor or invent dimensions. [2] [7]

## Evidence by feature

### Rear bench, five-seat identity, and split pattern

The 2020 Lexus brochure lists the RX 350’s standard “40/20/40-split folding, reclining second-row seat.” It separately identifies second-row captain’s chairs as an RX L/RXL option, which is useful exclusion evidence: the five-seat RX 350 target is the bench-seat vehicle, while the RX 350L is the longer three-row family and should not be used as a visual substitute. The same brochure lists the RX 350 AWD as a conventional two-row RX variant and the RX 350L AWD separately. [1]

The Canadian March 30, 2020 RX specification sheet identifies RX 350 seating capacity as 5 and lists “Rear Foldable 40/20/40 Seats” as standard, with “Power Folding Rear Seats 40/20/40” available in an Executive package. This is Canada-market documentation, so it confirms the architecture and the market-specific equipment distinction but should not be assumed to describe every U.S. package exactly. [3]

An exact-year Lexus dealer feature page identifies the rear seat as a “40-20-40 split bench,” manually reclining and sliding, with three adjustable rear headrests. The dealer page lists a five-person standard/max seating capacity and two seating rows. This is secondary catalog data rather than a factory manual, and the page warns that equipment can vary by vehicle. [4]

### Headrests and folding mechanism

The 2020 owner’s manual is the primary technical source. Before folding the rear seatbacks, it instructs the user to stow all rear head restraints and the rear center seat-belt buckle. It then identifies a seat-position adjustment lever and a seatback-angle adjustment lever. From inside, pulling the seatback-angle lever folds the seatback; from outside, the left lever folds the left rear seat and the right lever folds the right side and center rear seats. The center backrest also has a separate center-seatback angle lever behind the backrest. The backrests return by being lifted until they lock. [2]

The same manual states that the rear head restraints are part of the folding preparation and must be stowed rather than left extended. It does not provide a dimensional headrest drawing in the extracted text, so use photographs only for shape/appearance and not for geometry. [2]

### Center armrest

The exact-year dealer feature data identifies “rear folding, rear center with cupholders” armrests. The 2020 RX review from The Car Connection independently describes the bench as having a fold-down armrest. These sources establish the presence and general function of the center armrest; they do not establish its exact width, padding thickness, hinge construction, or whether every upholstery/package combination has identical trim. [4] [5]

The factory owner’s manual’s pictorial index labels an “Armrest” interior feature, but the extracted rear-seat folding pages focus on the seatback and belt procedure rather than detailing the armrest hinge. [2]

### Seat belts and child-restraint relationship

The owner’s manual requires the rear center seat-belt buckle to be stowed before folding and says that unused belts should be stored in the rear-seat side pockets. This directly establishes a belt-to-folding interaction: the buckles and webbing must be moved clear of the folding seatbacks. [2]

The manual says LATCH anchors are provided for the outer rear seats and that an anchor bracket is provided for each rear seat. It also identifies child-restraint use with either the LATCH system or a lap/shoulder belt. This is technical safety documentation, not a visual claim about the exact visible routing of every belt in every trim. [2]

The dealer feature data lists three-point belts at all rear positions, rear emergency-locking retractors, and rear belt pretensioners. Treat those as secondary specification data; the owner’s manual is the preferred source for operating and safety procedure. [4]

### Floor and cargo relationship

The exact-year Lexus dealer video page exposes a 2020 RX tutorial transcript and chapter list. The transcript describes a two-row vehicle, a cargo-side lever that folds the rear seats, a carpeted cargo-area mat, and a lift handle under the mat that accesses the spare tire/tools and storage. It also describes the cargo cover/tonneau cover and cargo-net attachment points. The page is inspectable as title, chapter metadata, and transcript, but this research did not independently inspect video frames. Therefore it is not used as visual proof of the seat or floor geometry. [7]

The Canadian specification sheet gives cargo capacity behind the second row of 694 L (24.5 cu. ft.) and behind the front seats of 1,580 L (55.8 cu. ft.) for the RX 350/RX 450h two-row models. The U.S. dealer page reports 18.4 cu. ft. with rear seats up and 56.3 cu. ft. with them down; the difference is a market/source measurement convention and should not be reconciled by inventing a floor dimension. [3] [4]

The manual’s folding instructions caution that passengers must not ride on a flattened seat and that the backrests must be lightly moved after folding to confirm that they are locked. This establishes a functional load-floor transition, but not that the result is perfectly flat relative to the cargo floor. [2]

## Visual-reference and rights notes

Image search surfaced exact-year listing/dealer images showing rear benches and cargo areas, including 2020 RX 350 AWD listings, but the search-result thumbnails alone were not promoted to evidence of a specific vehicle’s mechanism or trim. A thumbnail is not a provenance record, and the underlying listing/photo rights were not cleared. For an AutoLab visual reference package, use such images only after opening the original listing, recording the vehicle identity/VIN or explicit trim, preserving the original URL, and obtaining permission or using them only as internal research references.

The available evidence is sufficient for a technical reconstruction brief: five-seat bench, 40/20/40 segmentation, three adjustable rear headrests, center fold-down armrest/cupholders, reclining/sliding rear seat, three rear three-point belts, side-pocket belt stowage, seatback release levers, center-section release, and a carpeted cargo floor over underfloor storage. It is not sufficient to specify a flat-floor angle, exact hinge dimensions, belt anchor coordinates, foam/cover construction, or any other measurement.
